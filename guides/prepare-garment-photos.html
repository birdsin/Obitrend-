How to prepare a garment photo for an AI fashion workflow

A reference photo gives an image-generation system visual information to work from. If important garment details are hidden, blurred or affected by strong lighting, the generated result may guess those details. A careful reference makes review easier, although it cannot guarantee an exact reproduction.

1. Make the garment easy to see

  • Lay the garment flat or hang it naturally so its overall silhouette is visible.
  • Spread sleeves, trouser legs or straps where practical; avoid folding over the neckline, waistband or hem.
  • Keep hands, packaging, hangers and other objects from covering important construction details.
  • For a two-piece outfit, photograph both pieces clearly. If the pieces have different patterns or colours, make that distinction visible.

2. Use even, neutral lighting

Soft daylight near a window often shows colour and texture more reliably than a coloured lamp or direct flash. Avoid deep shadows, glare on shiny fabric and strong colour casts. If the garment is black, make sure the image still shows the edges and seams rather than turning the whole garment into a dark shape.

3. Include detail views when details matter

A full garment view helps communicate the overall shape, but small details may need closer views. Photograph the neckline, straps, buttons, pockets, waistband, stitching, embroidery, printed graphics and unusual trims separately when those features must remain recognisable. A single small image may not provide enough detail for every feature.

4. Keep the background simple

A plain background with clear contrast makes it easier to distinguish the garment edge from its surroundings. Avoid patterned bedding, clutter, similar-coloured walls or objects that overlap the clothing. Make sure the full garment fits inside the frame and is not cut off at the hem or sleeve.

5. Prepare a clean file

  • Use an image that is in focus and large enough to inspect.
  • Keep the original file so you can compare it with the generated result.
  • Do not add filters that change the garment's colour or texture.
  • Upload only photos you own or are authorised to use, especially if another person appears in the image.
Before you generate

Check the silhouette, colour, fabric, seams, neckline, straps, pockets, print and hem in the reference photo. Note any details that are partly hidden or difficult to distinguish. Those are the details you should inspect most carefully afterward.

What a better reference cannot guarantee

AI systems may still change colours, proportions, prints, fabric texture or construction. Use the generated image as a concept until you have checked it against the real garment. For product listings or paid campaigns, do not present an altered result as proof that the real item looks exactly the same.
