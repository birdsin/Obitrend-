How to check garment accuracy in an AI-generated fashion image

A fashion image can look polished and still misrepresent the clothing. Before using an AI-generated image in a shop, catalogue, campaign or social post, compare it with the original garment photo. Judge the clothing first; styling and background are secondary.

Use a side-by-side review

Keep the original reference open next to the generated image. Compare one garment feature at a time, zooming in where necessary. Do not rely on memory or on whether the overall image looks attractive.

1. Check the garment's identity

  • Category: Is a top still a top, and are trousers, shorts, a skirt or a dress still the same type of item?
  • Silhouette: Does the outline and length match the original?
  • Fit: Has the garment become much tighter, looser, longer or shorter?
  • Construction: Are the neckline, sleeves, straps, waistband, seams, buttons, pockets and hem in the right places?

2. Check colour and surface details

  • Colour: Compare the main colour in similar lighting. A dramatic background or colour grade can make differences harder to notice.
  • Prints and logos: Check the placement, scale, spelling, orientation and pattern. Generated text and logos may be inaccurate.
  • Fabric: Look for unexpected changes to texture, shine, thickness, drape or transparency.
  • Small details: Inspect decorative stitching, piping, belt loops, buttons, zips and other features that distinguish the product.

3. Check the image as a whole

Make sure the garment is not cropped where the full item is important. Check that limbs, hands, accessories or pose do not hide key details. Look for duplicated seams, distorted patterns, inconsistent shadows or parts of the garment blending into the background.

4. Decide how the image may be used

  • Concept board: A small difference may be acceptable if it is clearly treated as an idea rather than a product-accurate image.
  • Social campaign: Check that the garment remains recognisable and that the post does not make a false product claim.
  • Online store or catalogue: Use real product photographs where customers need to see the exact item. Do not use a generated image as the sole evidence of exact colour, fit or construction unless you have verified it against the actual product.
Simple decision rule

If a difference could change what a customer believes they are buying, correct the image, choose a more accurate photograph, or clearly disclose that it is an illustrative AI concept.

Keep a repeatable review record

For a collection, record the garment reference, output selected, any visible differences, the reviewer and the final decision. A short consistent checklist helps a team apply the same quality standard across multiple images.
