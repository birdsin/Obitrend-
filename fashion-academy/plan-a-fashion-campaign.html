How to plan a fashion campaign before the photoshoot

A campaign becomes easier to produce when its purpose is clear. Before choosing a location, model or editing style, write down what the images need to communicate and who needs to understand the message.

1. Define one campaign objective

Decide what the campaign should do. It might introduce a new collection, explain a signature garment, announce a seasonal edit or create awareness for a clothing label. Avoid giving one small campaign too many competing objectives. A clear goal helps you decide which images are essential and which are optional.

2. Describe the intended audience

Write a short description of the people you want to reach. Consider their style interests, the occasion they shop for, the questions they ask before buying and the platforms where they are likely to see the campaign. This is more useful than choosing a vague label such as “everyone who likes fashion.”

3. Build the visual story around the clothing

Choose three to five words that describe the mood, such as relaxed, architectural, romantic, energetic or minimal. Then check that the location, lighting, pose, styling and colour palette support those words. A dramatic background can look impressive, but it should not make the garment difficult to see.

4. Create a shot list

A practical shot list might include:

  • Hero image: the strongest overall image that communicates the campaign idea.
  • Full-length view: shows the complete silhouette and how the outfit is presented.
  • Detail view: highlights fabric, construction, print or a distinctive feature.
  • Context image: places the garment in a setting that supports the story.
  • Alternate crop: a composition suited to a specific social or web placement.

5. Plan for each placement

List where each image will appear: a website banner, product page, social feed, story, email or printed material. Record the required aspect ratio and safe space for text. Keep important garment details away from the crop edges. Do not assume one image can be cropped successfully for every placement.

6. Keep the campaign consistent

Use a small set of repeatable choices: a defined palette, a lighting direction, a background family and a consistent styling approach. If you are using AI-assisted image creation, keep notes on the reference image and settings used for each concept. Review each result against the original garment and against the campaign brief.

7. Review before publishing

Check spelling, logos, garment details, model presentation, image rights and platform requirements. Confirm that any AI-generated or altered image is not presented as an untouched photograph of a physical item. For retail, pair creative campaign imagery with accurate product photography and written specifications.

One-page brief: Objective · audience · garment or collection · three mood words · location/background · shot list · aspect ratios · rights and approvals · final accuracy check. If the brief cannot fit into these points, simplify the campaign before production.
