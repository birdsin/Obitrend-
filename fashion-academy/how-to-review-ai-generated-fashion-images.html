How to Review AI-Generated Fashion Images

A repeatable review method for checking garment accuracy, image quality, representation and suitability before publishing AI fashion visuals.

Compare against the original garment

Keep the original product photo open beside the generated result. Compare the silhouette, neckline, sleeve length, straps, hem, pockets, seams, closures, prints, logos and embellishments. Check both the overall outline and the small details that make the product recognisable.

Check colour and material appearance

Look for shifts in hue, brightness and saturation. A generated satin-like shine on a matte cotton garment can change how shoppers understand the product. Texture, drape and thickness can also be interpreted incorrectly. If the result changes a meaningful feature, do not use it as a factual product image.

Inspect the model and scene

Check hands, fingers, face, limbs, garment contact points, shadows and reflections. Make sure the pose does not hide important parts of the item. Review the background for visual artefacts, impossible objects, accidental logos or text that could be mistaken for real brand information.

Check framing and composition

For a full-body image, confirm that the entire model and outfit are visible when that is the purpose of the image. For a product detail, ensure the crop highlights the relevant feature without implying a different construction. Review the image at both phone and desktop sizes.

Decide what the image may be used for

A visually appealing concept may be suitable for mood boards or creative direction but not for a product detail page. Use verified photographs and specifications for factual product claims. Follow advertising, marketplace and consumer-protection requirements that apply to your business.

Simple pass-or-reject scorecard

  • Garment identity and major construction: pass / reject.
  • Colour and material representation: pass / reject.
  • Model anatomy and garment fit: pass / reject.
  • Background and image artefacts: pass / reject.
  • Crop, resolution and intended placement: pass / reject.
  • Disclosure and usage rights: pass / reject.

If any important category fails, regenerate or choose a different image rather than publishing a misleading result.

How to use this guide

Use the checklist as a starting point, then adapt it to the garment, audience and channel you are working with. Keep a record of the decisions that affect the final image so your next shoot or creative brief is easier to repeat.

AI image reminder: generated fashion images are visual concepts, not proof of a product's exact colour, construction, fit or material. Compare outputs with the real item before publishing product claims.

A structured quality-control process

Step 1: Establish the source of truth

Keep the original garment photograph and verified product information available during review. The generated image is not the authority on the product. Use a simple reference sheet listing the correct colour, material where known, garment category, construction features, available sizes and details that must not change. If the source information is uncertain, resolve that uncertainty before making a customer-facing claim.

Step 2: Review the garment in layers

First compare the overall silhouette and category. Next inspect construction: neckline, straps, sleeves, waistline, seams, pockets, closures, hem and panel placement. Finally inspect surface details such as prints, logos, embroidery, buttons and fabric texture. A result may look convincing at thumbnail size but reveal a changed pocket or missing strap when enlarged. Review at more than one scale.

Step 3: Check the model and physical interaction

Look for natural anatomy, plausible hands and feet, realistic joints, and believable contact between the garment and the body. Check whether fabric passes through the body, floats unnaturally or changes shape between limbs. Consider whether the pose hides an important feature or gives a misleading impression of length, fit or coverage. Reject images with visible distortions rather than assuming a viewer will overlook them.

Step 4: Inspect the scene and composition

Review shadows, reflections, furniture, signs, text, accessories and background objects. Look for duplicated objects, impossible edges, malformed lettering or accidental brand marks. Check the crop at the final display size and make sure the image has enough resolution for its intended use. A beautiful background does not compensate for an inaccurate product or an unusable crop.

Decide whether the image is suitable for its use

Use a stricter standard when an image is intended to help someone decide what to buy. A concept image may be appropriate for a mood board, internal creative discussion or clearly labelled campaign exploration. A product detail page needs accurate evidence of the actual item. Do not use a generated result to invent a material, fit, colour, size, feature or included accessory. Follow the disclosure requirements of the marketplace, advertising channel and jurisdiction where the image will appear.

Simple review scorecard

Score each category as Pass, Needs correction or Reject. A critical failure in garment identity or product truth should stop publication, even if the overall image looks attractive.

  • Garment identity: category, silhouette and key design details match the source.
  • Construction: seams, closures, straps, sleeves, pockets and hems are not materially changed.
  • Colour and surface: colour, print, logo and fabric appearance are not misleading.
  • Fit and anatomy: pose, body proportions and garment interaction look plausible.
  • Scene quality: no distracting artefacts, accidental text or misleading brand cues.
  • Composition: the intended garment view is visible and the resolution suits the channel.
  • Rights and disclosure: source material is authorised and any required disclosure is included.

Keep a record of review decisions

For each approved image, record the source garment reference, date, intended placement, reviewer and any corrections made. Save rejected results separately so they are not accidentally selected later. When a campaign contains multiple images, review the set together: colour, garment details, model styling and background continuity should be checked across the sequence, not just image by image.

What to do when a result fails

If the garment is wrong, return to the original reference and clarify the feature that changed. If the model or background contains artefacts, try a different pose or simpler scene. If the image remains inaccurate after reasonable revisions, use a real photograph instead. There is no requirement to publish every generated result. The purpose of review is to protect product accuracy and audience trust, not to find a reason to approve an unsuitable image.

Practical exercise: choose one generated image and compare it with the source using the seven scorecard categories. Ask another reviewer to repeat the check independently. Discuss any differences before deciding whether the image is suitable for its intended placement.
